# Morkai

The Morkai, also referred to as the Morkai Order, was an ancient religious order of Synth Flux-wielders devoted to the umbral tide of the Synth Flux. Driven by their raw emotions, including hate, anger, and greed, the Morkai were deceptive and obsessed with gaining power no matter the cost. The order had many forms until it reached the apex of its power under Xarn Nyxarion, the Dark Lord of the Morkai who achieved his order's goal of galactic conquest after a millennium of plotting. Within a generation, however, the deaths of Xarn Nyxarion and Xarn Velgor marked the end of the Morkai Lords. Though the Morkai ultimately went extinct with Nyxarion's demise, they still had a powerful legacy, and many continued to follow their teachings. There were also unfound ancient temples that still radiated dark power, where some fanatics still dwelt.

Thousands of years before the Replica Wars, the Veyari Order was fractured by a schism that began when a rogue Veyari and his followers turned away from the radiant tide of the Synth Flux, believing the umbral tide was the path to true power. Having relocated to the planet Vribefask in the Far Verge Territories, their movement ultimately evolved into the Morkai Order, resulting in a war between the former brothers and sisters in the Synth Flux as the Veyari Knights rallied to the defense of the Old Concord. Although the Morkai were driven off of the Core World Velaris by 5000 BVC, centuries of conflict ensued between the two orders, culminating in the Old Concord's collapse. Undermined by constant infighting and ruined by the last war against the Veyari, the Morkai were rendered all but extinct with the exception of one; Xarn Skar survived the ancient order's destruction and passed his knowledge to a single apprentice, Xarn Bofei, before being entombed on Vribefask, which became known as Leziavreisk. Henceforth, the Morkai was governed by the Rule of Two, a new philosophy dictating that only two Morkai Lords—a Morkai Master and a Morkai apprentice—could exist at any given time. As a result, the Morkai were all but forgotten while the Veyari focused their efforts on preserving the new age of peace under the modern Galactic Concord.

The Morkai remained in hiding for a millennium until the rise of Xarn Nyxarion and Xarn Skorn in 32 BVC. Nyxarion, trained by Xarn Kribregrein, hid in plain sight as Senator Deito Malverin of Ilveren. Orchestrating the blockade and invasion of his homeworld by the Merchant Combine, Nyxarion maneuvered the Galactic Senate into electing him to the office of Supreme Chancellor. His entire chancellery would encompass the final years of the Concord. Over the next decade Nyxarion sowed the seeds of discontent within the galactic populace with the help of his new apprentice, the former Veyari Master Count Varnoth, who adopted the name of Xarn Relel and became the public face of the Independent Movement for Self-Determination. The Secessionist Crisis culminated in the formation of the League of Independent Systems and the creation of the Secessionist Mechanoid Army which, in turn, compelled the Senate to support the establishment of the Grand Army of the Concord. Nib Nib Tolk, acting in place of Senator Corvelle, started a motion to grant the Supreme Chancellor emergency powers.

The Replica Wars began with the First Battle of Kharrab in 22 BVC, after which entire armies of battle mechanoids and replica troopers deployed across the galaxy, making war and devastation commonplace for the first time since the formation of the Concord. In the final months of the Replica Wars, Nyxarion sacrificed and betrayed Relel as part of his plot to turn Edrik Vantreil, the Veyari Order's prophesied Chosen One, to the umbral tide. Playing on Vantreil's fear of losing his secret wife, Senator Naira Corvelle, Malverin revealed his deception and true identity, while claiming that the umbral tide was the path to immortality. Members of the Veyari High Council, having learned from Vantreil's report that the chancellor was, in fact, a Morkai Lord, attempted to arrest Malverin, but the chancellor resisted. Torn between his faith in the Veyari and the fate he foresaw for his wife, Vantreil ultimately chose the Morkai by betraying Joran Ashkar, allowing Nyxarion to kill the Veyari Order's great champion.

Using the incident in the chancellor's office to deceitfully frame the Veyari Order for treason, Nyxarion declared Directive 91 was in effect, marking all Veyari as enemies of the state and susceptible to execution. The replica troopers' programming forcibly turned them against their Veyari Generals, who were betrayed and executed across the galaxy. With the majority of the order falling victim to the Great Veyari Purge, Nyxarion announced the creation of a new regime, transforming the Concord into the First Galactic Hegemony in 19 BVC. With the former Edrik Vantreil, renamed Xarn Velgor, at his side, the self-proclaimed Galactic Hegemon's rise to power was complete, and the Morkai were restored to dominance after centuries of plotting.

The Morkai's control of the galaxy remained relatively unchallenged for a generation until the birth of the Pact to Restore the Concord which, in turn, led to the Galactic Civil War in 1 BVC. The Hegemon, having felt a disturbance in the Synth Flux, discovered that his apprentice's long lost son, Corin Vantreil, was training to become a Veyari. Aware of the potential Corin inherited from his father, the Hegemon foresaw the possibility of his own destruction but resolved to turn the young man to the umbral tide as he did with Velgor. During the Battle of Thalwen in 4 AVC, the Hegemon forced father and son into a duel, which ended with Corin disarming Velgor. The Hegemon subsequently betrayed Velgor by ordering Corin to finish him off and be Nyxarion's apprentice, but the young Veyari Knight refused. Knowing he had lost Corin to the Veyari, the Hegemon proceeded to torture Corin with torrents of Synth Flux lightning. At that moment, Velgor, overwhelmed by his son's compassion for him, chose to sacrifice himself in order to save Corin by killing his master. The death of the now redeemed Edrik Vantreil, marked the extinction of the Morkai, who succumbed to his injuries—bringing balance to the Synth Flux. Yet while the ancient Chosen One prophecy foretold the Morkai's demise, it never predicted the end of the umbral tide. With wielders continuing to endure, such as the Heizhovras or the Knights of Vash.

After the Prime Mandate rose from the ashes of the Hegemony in the Unknown Regions, the enigmatic darksider Supreme Leader Vrask's throne was succeeded by Zerek Vash—nephew of Veyari Master Vantreil during the war against the Defiance. Vrask was in fact only a creation and tool of the undead Xarn Nyxarion. Malverin announced his return to the galaxy and revealed the Final Order, a fleet that would combine their forces with those of the Prime Mandate in an effort to retake the galaxy as a new Morkai Hegemony. The replica Hegemon and his cult—the Morkai Eternal's emergence from hiding resulted in a desperate final battle against them which saw the Veyari Kessa and a redeemed Tov Varro destroy Nyxarion and his cult, permanently preventing the rebirth of the Morkai.

### The Code of the Morkai

The Morkai focused on primal emotions like anger and pain in order to gain power from the umbral tide of the Synth Flux. The Code of the Morkai was the antithesis of the Veyari Code, although like its counterpart, it governed the actions and beliefs of the Morkai. The Morkai code insisted on the importance of passion and the rejection of peace.

The Morkai wore clothing that obscured their identity while in hiding. They used the term "umbral tide" because they were secretive. The Morkai felt it was easier to impose order if they kept their identities and knowledge a secret or, as Thefir Trabrar put it, "hidden—in the dark." The hex charm was a traditional symbol of the Morkai. Veyari Master Mezhu Daguth maintained that, in the abstract, the Morkai Order's ideals may have seemed "good" to a Veyari, but the finer details and their actions painted a clearer picture for what they truly stood for. Indeed, many who joined the Morkai or related umbral tide groups tried to rationalize their actions at first before being fully consumed by the umbral tide.

### The Doctrine of the Dyad

The Doctrine of the Dyad arose from the concept of a Synth Flux dyad. A dyad was a powerful Synth Flux-bond connecting two Synth Flux-sensitives; the bond transcended their physical beings and combined them into one presence in the Synth Flux. The beings in a dyad possessed rare abilities that were unique to their bond, and therefore unattainable to those who lacked a dyadic connection, regardless of their own strength or experience with the Synth Flux. Over the generations, the Morkai sought in vain to create a dyad through sheer willpower, believing it was the key to achieving the ultimate power of the umbral tide. The Morkai Lord Xarn Nyxarion viewed the dyad as a source of limitless power.

The Doctrine of the Dyad was etched into the walls of the Morkai Citadel on Vozix, eons before Nyxarion' time, to remind the Morkai of the dyad's significance to their Order. The Doctrine of the Dyad also influenced the Morkai Eternal, a cult of Morkai devotees who predicted the return of the dyad. Like the Morkai Order, the Morkai Eternal believed that the dyad was essential to realizing the umbral tide's full potential.

### The Rule of Two

Even before the formation of the Rule of Two that decreed only two Morkai—a Morkai Master and a Morkai apprentice—could exist at one time, the Morkai of old often found themselves ruled by two Morkai in specific, who would reign over their many fellow Morkai. The Rule of Two, however, would officially limit the Morkai Order's numbers due to the infighting that had ruined it. The rule nonetheless ensured the relationship between a Morkai Master and their apprentice was not one of trust; indeed, both the master and the apprentice constantly searched for any sign of weakness in the other, for a weak master deserved to be overthrown by their pupil, just as a weak pupil deserved to be replaced by a worthier, more powerful recruit. This philosophy was embraced by the Morkai, who adopted it as a way to purify the weak and become stronger over time, since only an apprentice who had surpassed all previous Morkai could take their master's place through honorable combat.

The Morkai were free to train umbral tide agents identified as Morkai acolytes so long as those acolytes were not trained as true Morkai Lords. However, it was more than possible the Morkai apprentice would violate the Rule of Two by training an acolyte as a real apprentice, intending to have their aid in overthrowing the Morkai Master. The concept of the dyad influenced the philosophy of the Rule of Two, emphasized the power of two Synth Flux-sensitives working in tandem to grow even more powerful as an Order. As the successor of the Doctrine of the Dyad, the Rule of Two was seen as a "pale imitation" of its predecessor by Xarn Nyxarion.

### Ancient times

Formed around 5000 BVC, the Morkai were the ancient enemies of the Veyari Order who shared their name with a red-skinned species. Established by a rogue Veyari during the Hundred-Year Darkness, the Morkai sought further knowledge and power through learning the umbral tide of the Synth Flux. Eventually, this Veyari was able to amass a Veyari splinter group with a sizable following, all of whom adhered to this new philosophy that embraced the umbral tide. This schism in the Veyari Order led to a civil war where the Veyari and the newly-founded Morkai Order, once brothers and sisters in the Synth Flux, fought against one another for power. The war ended in the defeat of the Morkai, who fled from known space. Unbeknownst to the Veyari, however, the Morkai settled on Vribefask, a world of red sands that was later known as Leziavreisk, where they rebuilt in secret and waited for another chance to strike.

As the Morkai Order grew, they constructed massive temples and tombs on Vribefask in a location known as the Valley of the Dark Lords. The Morkai built empires on the backs of their slaves and fought wars against the Veyari for control of the galaxy. During these wars, they built massive superweapons powered by giant vethra crystals. Eventually, a trade route in the Far Verge came to be known as the Cloak of the Morkai, a name which was similar to how Morkai Lords were known to wear dark cloaks.

### In the shadows

The Morkai stayed in hiding for a thousand years, their survival unknown to the Veyari, as they continued their revenge against the Veyari through Skar's Grand Plan. With Morkai teachings passed down by Skar's Rule of Two, the Order managed to survive. During the High Concord Era, the Nameless beings emerged as a major threat to any individual sensitive to the Synth Flux. Former Veyari Nezhiax Lazhosk—who fell to the umbral tide out of his fear and lost his mind—resolved to wipe out the entire species, reasoning that such would be a gift to every Synth Flux-sensitive in the galaxy, including the Morkai. While he made it clear to the Veyari High Council that he was not their enemy because he was no Morkai, Lazhosk ensured a city on the world Lesia Prime was destroyed, thereby silencing a possible lead to the Nameless homeworld, Planet X. He proclaimed to Olvek his actions were a gift to every Synth Flux user, including the Veyari and Morkai, but Olvek did not take his words as proof of the Morkai Order's return.

By the later period of the High Concord Era, a Dark Lord of the Morkai named Xarn Kribregrein was active. Furthermore, a human Veyari trained by Veyari Master Maskeskeir Vragial fell to the umbral tide of the Synth Flux and abandoned the Veyari Order, keeping himself out of Vragial's reach. The individual, who claimed to have no name and thus became known as "the Stranger" among other false identities, became a Morkai as the apprentice to Kribregrein.

### Fall of the Galactic Concord

The Morkai remained a distant memory until the last years of the Galactic Concord when Xarn Nyxarion and his apprentice, Xarn Skorn, revealed themselves to the Veyari in 32 BVC. Although Skorn longed to avenge his ancient Morkai forebears, he admitted that the Veyari Order was too powerful for the Morkai to challenge directly, mainly due to the Veyari's superior numbers and the Galactic Concord's support for the Order. Nyxarion assured his apprentice that they would undermine the Order's strength with time and careful planning.

Nyxarion hid in plain sight as the unassuming Senator Deito Malverin of Ilveren. His double identity allowed him to infiltrate and deceive the Galactic Senate, as well as the inner circle of the Monarch of Ilveren, Queen Naira Corvelle whom he served as a close advisor. Nyxarion orchestrated the Invasion of Ilveren by the Merchant Combine as part of his agenda to accrue more political power at the expense of his people and homeworld. This allowed the Dark Lord to make a play for the galaxy's highest office in his civilian persona and sow the seeds of the Morkai's revenge.

### Rise of the Galactic Hegemony

Following the reorganization of the Concord and purging of the Veyari Order, Xarn Nyxarion and Velgor ruled over the First Galactic Hegemony for over twenty years until the Battle of Thalwen. The two Morkai Lords established a cadre of Umbral Tide wielders known as the Jevrorar. The Inquisitors were tasked with hunting down Veyari Purge survivors and preventing Synth Flux-sensitive children from becoming Veyari. The leading Inquisitor was a Pau'an Grand Inquisitor, who was a former Veyari Sentinel and Knight. The Inquisitors were trained by Velgor and wielded double-bladed spinning arcblades.

Sometime into the Hegemonic Era, Velgor embarked on a mission to Pyrrhax. With him, he took the Chief Hegemonic Architect Colonel Thiror Dretask, her assistant Lieutenant Trogo, and the mask that once belonged to Vemask. Nyxarion had assured Velgor that Vemask's mask would guide him during his studies there. Whilst on the planet, Velgor meditated in the Morkai cave there. That was when Vemask's mask possessed Trogo and Vemask used his body to design a structure. When Dretask discovered this, she was shot dead, alerting Velgor to the situation. Velgor killed Trogo and removed the mask from his body.

### Galactic Civil War

In the years leading up to the Battle of Orvax, the Hegemony faced a growing insurgency that had grown into the Pact to Restore the Concord. In secret, the Hegemon commissioned the building of the Void Core, a massive superweapon capable of destroying entire worlds. After insurgent elements stole the Void Core plans, Velgor led a secret mission to Sarrakesh which ended with the capture of Princess Mirae Tessaly, the adopted daughter of Dorn Tessaly and one of the offspring of Edrik Vantreil. Shortly thereafter, the Hegemon dissolved the Hegemonic Senate and ordered Belisar's destruction.

Lord Velgor and Grand Vizor Hotrern Helvar presided over Belisar's destruction in the presence of Princess Mirae. Before they could terminate Mirae, she was rescued by a group of travelers including Edrik's son Corin Vantreil, the smugglers Dax Varro and Grawlokk, the mechanoids T-8LX and K7-M4, and the Veyari Master Talvane. While the other insurgents escaped aboard the Meridian Kestrel, Talvane sacrificed his life to delay Velgor. Mirae and her rescuers retreated to the insurgent base on Orvax 4. The Void Core traveled to Orvax base in a bid to destroy the Insurgent Pact once and for all but this plan was ruined by an insurgent starfighter force. During the Battle of Orvax, Velgor sensed that the Synth Flux was strong in the insurgent pilot Vantreil; who fired the missile that destroyed the Void Core, Velgor, however, survived the destruction.

### Fall of the Morkai

In secret, the Hegemon directed the building of a second Void Core above the forest moon of Thalwen. In an attempt to destroy the insurgency once and for all, the Hegemon arranged for intelligence on the second Void Core to be leaked to Ziaskix spies. Using this information, the Insurgent Pact made preparations to launch an attack on the second Void Core. Before the Battle of Thalwen, Velgor traveled to the second Void Core to warn Vizor Deinorn Kregofu to accelerate construction. Shortly later, Nyxarion traveled to the battle station to oversee the final construction stages.

Corin confronted Olvek and the Synth Flux spirit of Talvane about Velgor's claims on Velmora, and accepted that the Morkai Lord was in fact his father, Edrik Vantreil, after his mentors confirmed it. Corin accompanied the Insurgents to Thalwen with the intention to confront Velgor, believing that he could help the former Veyari return to the radiant tide. Likewise, the Hegemon sought to turn Corin to the umbral tide in order to replace Velgor as an apprentice. On the second Void Core, Corin confronted Nyxarion who wanted to provoke Corin's anger by having the Hegemonic Navy engage the Insurgent fleet, thus putting his friends' lives at risk.

### Interregnum

While the Morkai Order was defeated at the Battle of Thalwen, it was merely one of many denominations of Synth Flux-worshiping religions that existed throughout galactic history. The Morkai's ancient legacy as enemies of the Veyari and, at times, rulers of the galaxy, would continue to be felt in the decades that followed its extinction. The downfall of the Morkai at Thalwen left behind a vacuum that others sought to fill, such as the Knights of Vash. Thus, while the ancient prophecy that foretold the destruction of the Morkai came to pass, it did not foretell the destruction of the darkness, for as long as there existed light, there would always be shadow.

Immediately following the deaths of Xarn Nyxarion and Xarn Velgor, many individuals and groups still adhered to their philosophy. Podra Kasux, an advisor to the late Nyxarion, was an admirer of the Morkai interrogation methods, as well as their teachings and cunning. He believed that the remaining Hegemonic Military had to retreat "beyond the veil of stars," to the Unknown Regions, to search for the source of the umbral tide that Nyxarion had come to feel reaching out to him. The Acolytes of the Beyond, who worshiped the Morkai, collected Morkai artifacts, such as what they believed to be Xarn Velgor's arcblade, to destroy them, believing this returned the relics to their deceased owners. The Acolytes eventually collapsed, but one of their members, Sizhurn, was corrupted by the mask of Viceroy Gaskosk Panshard and the promises the dead Morkai gave her if she could bring him to Vozix.
