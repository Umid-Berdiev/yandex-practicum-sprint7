# Galactic Hegemony

The Galactic Hegemony, also known as the First Galactic Hegemony, the Hegemonic regime, Malverin's regime, or simply the Hegemony, and later as the Old Hegemony, was a totalitarian stratocratic fascist dictatorship that replaced the Galactic Concord at the end of the Replica Wars. Though the Hegemony rose to power by promising to bring order to the galaxy, it oversaw a period of military rule and aggressive expansion. The subsequent Hegemonic Era would last only two decades, for it was eventually overthrown by the Pact to Restore the Concord in the Galactic Civil War, but not before the sovereign star systems of the galaxy's known regions had been largely conquered by the Hegemony's forces.

The Hegemony traced its origins to a Morkai conspiracy to destroy the Veyari Order and regain control over the galaxy. The Hegemony was secretly controlled by the Morkai, for the Galactic Hegemon was, in fact, the Morkai Lord Xarn Nyxarion, whose reign was enforced by his apprentice, Xarn Velgor, whom he eventually elevated to the position of supreme commander of the Hegemonic fleet. Rising to power on a groundswell of populist support and capitalizing on anti-Veyari and anti-mechanoid sentiment and human-ethnocentrism manufactured by the destructive Replica Wars, Xarn Nyxarion appointed himself Hegemon at the end of the conflict by promising security and stability to the war-torn Concord. Gaining the overwhelming approval of the Galactic Senate, Xarn Nyxarion staged a coup by the Veyari and oversaw their subsequent purge from the galaxy, assisted by his right-hand, Xarn Velgor.

The Hegemony stood virtually unchallenged during its rise to power, with the backing of the Hegemonic Senate and the spreading of its massive technological military force across the galaxy and began a major expansion and encroaching into the unexplored regions. At first, the Galactic Hegemony was a constitutional monarchy in basic theory, however it gradually evolved into a stratocracy as the power of the Hegemonic Senate waned. However, numerous insurgent cells sprang up against the Hegemony and gradually united to initiate a growing insurgency, winning its first major victory over Pyrrhax in 4 BVC. These insurgent groups eventually formed the Insurgent Pact in 2 BVC, and achieved its first major victory with the theft of the Void Core plans on Quubem, which, in turn, led to the destruction of the Void Core at the Battle of Orvax. The following years saw initial Pact successes until a major Hegemonic victory on Kryost in 3 AVC, but the Hegemony suffered a devastating defeat one year later at the Battle of Thalwen. During the battle, Xarn Nyxarion was slain at the hands of his former disciple, the redeemed Edrik Vantreil, who himself died shortly thereafter, marking the end of Morkai rule and the deterioration of the Hegemony and the resumption of the Galactic Concord, the New Concord.

With the deaths of the Hegemon and his top lieutenant, factionalism overcame the Hegemony as it fractured into breakaway fiefdoms led by splinter Vizors, admirals, and generals, many of whom ignored Grand Vizier Mas Traga's authority at the capital and became warlords as the Hegemony continued to fight the Concord. The Hegemonic Navy came to be publicly led by loyalist Grand Admiral Libres Migran, though she was a figurehead for Fleet Admiral Jeshesk Venn. Ultimately, Venn seized control over the loyalist forces, removing Migran from power and becoming Hegemon-in-effect through his new position as Counselor to the Hegemony. Under Venn's command, the Hegemony made its final stand at the Battle of Leinesk in 5 AVC, ultimately losing and capitulating to the New Concord. With the cessation of the fighting, the Galactic Concordance was signed, vanquishing the Hegemony.

While the Hegemony was gone, the New Concord faced continued resistance from several Hegemonic remants in the years after. Eventually its successor, the Prime Mandate began a clandestine armament program to rebuild the former Hegemonic Military. The miltary junta was unaware, however, that they had been manipulated by a new hidden conspiracy, for secret cultists on the lost world of Vozix attended to the spirit of Xarn Nyxarion, and constructed a fleet built to turn the Prime Mandate into a true empire. The cult efforts ultimately failed, however, for they were opposed by the Defiance and Veyari Order, leading to an end of the Prime Mandate.

### Early foundations

The Hegemonic ideals of the Galactic Hegemony could be dated back to the Morkai Hegemony of the Old Concord's time period. Following the defeat of the Morkai during their final war with the Veyari Order, for at least a thousand years, the dominant governing body of the galaxy was the unicameral, parliamentary, and democratic Galactic Concord, which was governed by the Galactic Senate and led by an elected Supreme Chancellor.

In the last decades of its existence, the Concord Senate became embroiled in bureaucracy, rendering it woefully ineffectual as a governing body. Xarn Nyxarion, the Dark Lord of the Morkai known publicly as Senator Deito Malverin of Ilveren, privately lamented the declining state of the Senate, noting that the Concord was "not what it once was."

### Birth of the New Order

Since the onset of the Replica Wars in 22 BVC, the Concord endeavored to strengthen its security and stability through the creation of new organizations such as the Commission for the Protection of the Concord (LXDRTM), which utilized propaganda to increase public morale, promote patriotism, and foster anti-Secessionist sentiment within the general population. As the Concord became increasingly centralized, however, Chancellor Malverin's powers continued to grow through additional assets ceded to the Office of the Chancellor, including the galactic banking system that was placed under his direct oversight. This gave Nyxarion the finance he needed to build his empire. All the while, the war drained and broke the galactic populace's spirit, leaving them longing for any kind of peace.

The Chancellor remained a popular leader for the duration of the war, having managed to stay in office at the will of the Senate for longer than his term permitted. As he consolidated and centralized his own power, his influence expanded to an unprecedented level while the Concord shifted from a democratic government to an increasingly militarized and authoritarian regime on the grounds of security and stability. In addition to his authority as Commander-in-Chief of the Concord Military, the Chancellor exerted more control over the Senate, the courts, and even the media, with Nitiarn News becoming a state-run program in order to ensure that information would not be compromised by the League of Independent Systems. Nyxarion's hold over the Concord further expanded with additional amendments to the Galactic Constitution while public support for the Veyari weakened as the war carried on, paving the way for his ultimate ascension as Hegemon of the galaxy.

### Imperialization

Existing government bodies were renamed to reflect the change in authority as civil and military superstructure of the Old Concord were overhauled immediately under the personal direction of the Hegemon. With vast portions of the galaxy still unconquered, the Grand Army of the Concord became the Hegemonic Army, while the Concord Navy became the Hegemonic Navy and the Galactic Senate was rechristened the Hegemonic Senate. The renowned Senate Plaza, with its name reminding residents of the Old Concord, was promptly changed to the Hegemonic Plaza. Ultimately, the mechanisms created for the Concord during the war were twisted to fit Nyxarion' use, with even the systems built with help from the Veyari benefiting the new Hegemon. All the while, the Hegemon and his followers began formulating their century plan for the galaxy and how it would change under Hegemonic rule.

In addition, the former roundel of the Concord was replaced with the new Hegemonic crest, adorned on almost everything having to do with Nyxarion' Hegemony. With every day that passed, Nyxarion' hold over the galaxy grew tighter. For the next decade and a half, Hegemonic rule—bolstered by the military created to fight in the Replica Wars—dominated the galaxy and was largely unchallenged. The Hegemony soon began to upgrade its military hardware, replacing V-wing and ARC-170 fighters with newer VEX models five years after the Replica Wars, but Concord-era military equipment continued to be used for a while on distant outposts and locations such as Puvidre Station. The Concord's Phase II replica trooper armor would begin a phase out and be replaced by superior shocktrooper armor, whose development had been headed by the Hegemonic Department of Military Research. In the meantime, the Hegemony quietly supported the activity of the 4040s activist group, whose members came to function as something of a paramilitary.

### Age of the Hegemony

In 19 BVC, Hegemonic forces arrived on the planet Goke in the Free Trade sector to bring it under the Hegemony's control. On Rix Road, a regiment of replica troopers under an Hegemonic officer marched pass the gathered masses of civilians as a show of Hegemonic authority, although local citizen Clem Kriasi assured his adoptive son Teodric Jiadrax Kriasi that the Hegemony would pull out soon enough. To Clem, resisting the Hegemony was not their fight. However, a small group of protesters emerged from the crowd and began to throw rocks at the soldiers in the name of the Concord and Goke in what became known as the Rix Road protest. Believing they would only make things worse for themselves, Clem quickly stepped in to stop the protest, only for the officer to call his troopers to a halt at that moment. Pointing their pulsers at the protesters and Clem, who was assumed to be part of the demonstration, the protest was shut down. Clem was publicly hung on Rix Road for his supposed treason.

Afterward, Teodric attacked four replica troopers stationed near the gallows in anger. At some point, Kriasi spent time with anti-Hegemonic anarchist groups. Ultimately, Teodric would be arrested on charges of attacking an Hegemonic soldier, insurrection, and destroying Hegemonic property. After spending time in prison, the sixteen year old Kriasi was sent to the planet Skushiarn, which had been an ally to the Concord during the Replica Wars but possessed vital resources, as a cook for the Hegemonic Army. After six months on Skushiarn, Kriasi escaped by deserting the military. He later claimed he had seen active combat. Upon escaping, Kriasi realized the Hegemony had set him and his other conscripts against people who should have been their allies. Although Kriasi's resolve to fight the Hegemony was damaged, the people of Skushiarn, the Skushiarnese, resisted the Hegemony's encroachment on their world as the Hegemonic Era carried on. Kriasi ultimately returned home to Goke, which largely remained out of direct Hegemonic control and instead was governed by the Vuros-Zogrei corporate polity on behalf of the central Hegemonic regime. Despite his hate for the Hegemony, Kriasi's spirit was crushed, and he attempted to eke out a life for himself, his mother Bovask, and their mechanoid B2EMO.

### Combating the growing insurgency

By 5 BVC, the Galactic Hegemony had made plans for the expansion of the Zhako dam involving the enslavement of many Thadosk, with Engineering officer Colonel Thidri Gigern placed in charge of overseeing the next steps. Despite the Hegemonic-supported viewing event in the Enterprise Zone drawing many Thadosk away from viewing the revered celestial event known as the "Eye of Zhako" in the the sacred Valley of Caves, the site of the Zhako dam, some Thadosk persisted in traveling to the valley. For those Thadosk that still decided to make the ten-day trek to the valley of caves, the Hegemony's Zhako Garrison, under the command of commandant Kreitim Thiakom and aware that the Thadosk would refuse, offered transport to the valley from the Lowlands and established Comfort Units along the route, convincing many to abandon the long journey and eventually dwindling the number of pilgrims from five hundred to around sixty.

That same year, Colonel Gigern visited the dam to inspect it for the expansion project, which commandant Thiakom viewed as a way off Zhako for him and his family. Around that time, VEX/ln space superiority starfighters from Beheirn Air Base, an airbase located fifty-two klicks from the Zhako dam, performed patrol flights across Zhako's Highlands, with one starfighter passing but not spotting the insurgent freedom fighters Tujas Niavrim and Teodric Kriasi. Later, another another Hegemonic pilot performed a flyby in their VEX fighter past what they thought was a farm, which in reality, was a camp serving as a base of operations for the group of insurgents Kriasi and Niavrim were a part of, which planned to steal the quarterly payroll of Hegemonic troops in the Shokradriax sector stored at the Zhako dam. During the planning stages of the heist, one of the team's members, Skeho Zases, wrote a maniesto that would later be spread galaxy-wide in which Zases theorized that the Hegemony's need for control was unnatural, its authority was brittle, its oppression was the mask of fear, and as a result, soon enough, the Hegemony would be overrun with defiance and its control would break.

### Galactic Civil War

Soon after its founding, the Insurgent Pact pressed on with the planned operation on Heifu by stealing clearance codes from the Hegemonic Security Bureau station on Diagux 71 to allow their forces to mount a successful attack on the Hegemony. However, the planned insurgent attack on Heifu did not go forward thanks to Vyrell, who had discovered the location of Rivet Base, the headquarters of the Phoenix Group. Vyrell's fleet blockaded Grobe and inflicted heavy casualties on insurgent forces. Vyrell launched a ground assault after an orbital bombardment. Meanwhile, Skagruth, who had escaped the fighting, enlisted the help of Clan Aldis.

In response, Governor Sibirn dispatched Reradrein, but these reinforcements failed to stop the Kordathians from destroying the last Interdictor cruiser. Despite Vyrell storming Rivet Base, the insurgents and Hegemonics were attacked by an angry Skothe, an enigmatic being that was living on Grobe, who used his Synth Flux powers to devastate the insurgent base before being subdued by Vyrell. The surviving insurgent and Kordathian forces fled the battle as the Hegemony wiped out the Phoenix Squadron; however, the Hegemony was still unaware of the true scale of the Insurgent Pact. Nonetheless, the routing at Grobe was seen as proof by the insurgents that they were not yet ready for open war with the Hegemony.

### Fragmentation of the Order

With the destruction of the Void Core and death of the Order's totalitarian ruler, many worlds across the galaxy began to celebrate the Hegemony's defeat over Thalwen with the incorrect notion that the war was over and rise up against the regime. Despite being at the heart of the Hegemony, the Hegemonic Center would also see tumultuous festivities by its populace, eventually leading to a riot breaking out in Monument Plaza, which further coalesced into a full-scale civil war. A rally was held on Ilveren while parades and other celebrations were held on Sarrakesh, which saw its Hegemonic occupiers withdraw. The recent death of Gorvo the Zogg, combined with the Hegemonic withdraw, did create a power vacuum on Sarrakesh that groups like the Mining Collective could take advantage of, however.

Meanwhile, the Hegemony rushed to reassert its power. One particular tactic the Hegemony would focus on was trying to keep control over resources. To that end, the Hegemony continued its secret mining project on Haron. The Insurgent Pact also suspected Hegemonic holdouts would dig into the Far Verge, thereby drawing out the war. Beyond fearing covert attacks on Pact-allied star systems, with the number of pro-Pact worlds growing in the wake of Malverin's death, and the battles that were to be waged in the Far Verge, the Pact feared the Hegemony would act in the shadows to destabilize the bedrock upon which the New Concord would be formed: assassinations, starvation, and control over resources like fuel, such as Tibanna gas, and food were all tactics the Pact suspected would be used by the Hegemony in the coming weeks.

### Hegemony's End

Despite the post-Thalwen Insurgent Pact's fears that the Hegemony would dig into the Far Verge, the Hegemony was largely defeated in the region by the time Venn called for a meeting of select Hegemonics in the Piapazhur Nebula. The Exterior under Grand Vizor Randd remained the only Far Verge sector that could truly be described as under the Hegemony's control. However, while the Hegemony appeared to be receding as the New Concord grew in size, Venn consolidated more and more systems to his cause—a faction of Hegemonics identified as the loyalists—after staging the Hegemonic Future Council to eliminate his rivals. A protégé of the late Hegemon, Venn saw the Hegemony of old as weak, corrupt, and overripe. To him, by allowing the New Concord to gain ground, he not only eliminated potential competition but consolidated the leftover pieces of the Order.

With the shipyards of Briakrix, Piban Noquoth, Meimeis, and Turco Prime already lost or contested, Venn planned to use the Hegemony's last foothold in the Far Verge as a "strangling cord" to tie around the New Concord. While the old Hegemony relied on third-party corporations to produce its military hardware, Venn had nationalized all aspects of the production chain to be solely Hegemonic. To conclude his plan, Venn founded the Shadow Council in the Piapazhur Nebula after rescuing the final member of the council, Treibrisk Mazen, from Quosil. This "Shadow Council" was to serve as a secret advisory board composed of the "best and brightest," with the ultimate motive of secretly guiding the Hegemony into a new golden age.

### Legacy

As outlined in the Galactic Concordance, a new future for the Hegemony was granted to remaining Hegemonic forces within a predetermined territory of the galaxy that included parts of the Core Worlds, Colonies, and the Near Verge, containing many ancient and conservative worlds that had supported Malverin including Vrotatharn, Gratridrisk, Brudro, Krelisk, and Pevreirn. As such, at the command of the New Concord, all Hegemonic forces and remnants that complied with the provisions of the concordance retreated within the new boundaries.

However, the leadership of the Inner systems' Hegemonic remnant, made up of the various fragmented successors to the Hegemony, quickly devolved into petty squabbles on all matters from relations with the New Concord to which Hegemonic legacies deserved preservation, which combined with the regulation and dismemberment of the once-mighty Hegemonic military-industrial complex, resulted in struggle to turn the faction into a viable state. As a result, the Inner systems' Hegemonic remnant disintegrated soon after its founding, leading to several of its key worlds joining the New Concord instead, the representatives and leaders of which went on to become leaders of the Centrists, a political party which advocated and pushed for a stronger central government and military.

### Government

The First Galactic Hegemony was a fascist dictatorship led by a Galactic Hegemon and enforced by a vast Hegemonic Military. The Hegemon acted as the sovereign ruler and ultimate authority in all political and military matters of state. Aiding the Hegemon was a Grand Vizier, who was the head of the Hegemonic Ruling Council, which in turn consisted of advisors who wielded considerable powers over all aspects of the Hegemony's political and military apparatus. While supposedly bound by the Hegemonic Charter, Hegemonic rulers were largely above the law.
