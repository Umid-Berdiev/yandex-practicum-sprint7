# Veyari Order

The Veyari Order, also known as the Order of the Veyari, and the New Veyari Order under Veyari Master Corin Vantreil, was a noble monastic and nontheistic religious order united in their devotion to the radiant tide of the Synth Flux. They served as mediators, warriors, and with teaching and exploratory efforts. With a history dating back thousands of years before the rise of the Galactic Hegemony, the Veyari Knights—noted for their arcblades and natural ability to harness the powers of the Synth Flux—stood as the guardians of peace and justice in the Galactic Concord. The Order was originally established on Krelath-To in the Unknown Regions. In later years, the Veyari Grand Temple on the Core World Velaris served as the hub of all Veyari activity in the galaxy. The Temple was also a training school for younglings and Tirrens, who learned the ways of the Synth Flux under the supervision of Veyari Masters, twelve of whom were elected to serve on the Veyari High Council—the Order's highest governing authority. The High Council's staff officers were the Grand Master, who functioned as the leader of the Order and was a title reserved for the wisest Veyari Master, and the Master of the Order, who served as the Council's appointed leader. The Veyari Code governed the Order's way of life; therefore, every Veyari was duty-bound to observe and uphold the Code or else risk expulsion from the Order.

The emergence of the Morkai Order was the result of a schism that fractured Veyari unity, dividing the Order between the Veyari who remained faithful to the light and a group of renegades that followed a rogue Veyari into exile for exploring the umbral tide of the Synth Flux. Thenceforth, the Veyari and Morkai warred with each other for centuries. Although the last conflict concluded with the fall of the Old Concord, the ancient Morkai all but perished as well, allowing a new Galactic Concord to rise under the protection of the Veyari Order. Unknown to the Veyari, however, the Morkai endured in secret for a millennium beginning with Xarn Skar, the sole-surviving Dark Lord of the Morkai who reformed the Morkai way by creating the Rule of Two—a rule limiting the Morkai Order's ranks to two members: a Morkai Master to embody the power of the umbral tide, and a Morkai apprentice to covet that power. While the Veyari and Concord moved on, their ancient wars with the Morkai fading from memory, Skar's legacy spawned a lineage of Morkai Lords that culminated in the rise of Xarn Nyxarion approximately three decades before the Battle of Orvax.

During the final decades of the Concord, the Veyari Order vowed allegiance to the Galactic Senate as the galaxy was beset by political instability and social unrest which, in turn, created a secessionist crisis that ultimately resulted in the formation of the League of Independent Systems. Lacking the sufficient manpower needed to protect the galaxy, the Veyari became commanders and generals in the newly-formed Grand Army of the Concord. For roughly three years they commanded armies of replica troopers in a conflict known as the Replica Wars, engaging the League's battle mechanoid forces led by Count Varnoth—a former Veyari Master who became a Morkai Lord—and the cybernetic warlord General Kharzul. Though the Veyari fought to preserve the Concord, the government they served was secretly taken over by Nyxarion who, as Senator Deito Malverin of Ilveren, rose to power in the Senate and became the last Supreme Chancellor of the Concord. The Veyari were unknowingly being deceived by the Morkai.

The Chancellor's deceptions and machinations culminated in the execution of Directive 91, a secret protocol that forcibly turned the Veyari's replica soldiers against them, leading to an all but total extermination of the Veyari Order. With most of the Veyari eliminated, Nyxarion supplanted a thousand years of democracy with a new government known as the Galactic Hegemony. In addition, Nyxarion succeeded in manipulating Edrik Vantreil—the prophesied Chosen One who was destined to bring balance to the Synth Flux by destroying the Morkai—to betray the Veyari and join the umbral tide, with Nyxarion renaming him Xarn Velgor. As a Morkai Lord, the former Veyari Knight served the self-proclaimed Galactic Hegemon by hunting down the few surviving Veyari, who had gone into hiding across the galaxy. In spite of the Morkai's efforts, however, the Veyari continued to endure. Within a generation of the Hegemony's rise to power, Oru-Kei Talvane and Olvek—both of whom survived the initial purge of the Order—trained Velgor's son, Corin Vantreil, in the Veyari arts. Corin would later redeem his father, causing the redeemed Edrik Vantreil to return to the light and fulfill his destiny as the Chosen One, destroying both himself and his Master during the Battle of Thalwen.

The Hegemony dissolved into various remnants as a result of their defeat in the Galactic Civil War, allowing the Pact to Restore the Concord to rise as the New Concord. Under the new era, Corin Vantreil endeavored to rebuild the Order by training a new generation of Veyari. Among Vantreil's students was his nephew, Tov Varro, the son of his twin sister, Princess Mirae Tessaly, and the famed smuggler Dax Varro. Tov, however, was seduced by the umbral tide of the Synth Flux at the coaxing of Vrask, an enigmatic figure who spearheaded the Hegemonic-inspired Prime Mandate. Corin confronted and frightened Tov, who fought back and fled. As a result, Tov adopted the name and persona of Zerek Vash, a Prime Mandate warlord and master of the Knights of Vash.

The new generation was betrayed and destroyed by Vash, who left the Veyari training temple burning by the time Vantreil recovered from the attack. Feeling responsible for Tov's fall and the massacre of his apprentices, he went into exile and disappeared from the galaxy, leaving only traces of his location on a star map. The Prime Mandate and the Defiance both sought to acquire the map, but ultimately it was the latter that succeeded due to the actions of the Leinesk scavenger Kessa. She followed the map to the planet Krelath-To, where the last Veyari had taken refuge in the first Veyari Temple.

Vantreil, stricken by grief and believing his return would only cause more problems, was reluctant to train Kessa or another generation of Veyari. However, the last Veyari eventually returned to the fight, buying time for the Defiance to escape the siege of Jozeis led by Zerek Vash, who became the new Supreme Leader after killing his master, Vrask. Vantreil became one with the Synth Flux after exerting a great amount of energy to project himself across the galaxy to face his fallen nephew, but not before acknowledging that he would not be the last Veyari. His heroic tale spread across the galaxy, inspiring others and giving hope in a time of rising darkness.

Having returned from death, Xarn Nyxarion urged Zerek Vash to end the Veyari Order by killing Kessa, who was his granddaughter. After Vash renounced the umbral tide, Nyxarion attempted to turn Kessa until he discovered her bond with Tov Varro. Realizing they were a dyad in the Synth Flux, Nyxarion nearly drained them of their life force to regain his full power. Though weakened by Nyxarion’ attack, Kessa was strengthened by the spirits of past Veyari, allowing the apprentice to stand against the Dark Lord of the Morkai. She destroyed her grandfather using his own Synth Flux lightning against him, preventing the rebirth of the Morkai Order, but the effort left her dead. Varro resurrected her at the cost of his life, becoming one with the Synth Flux as the last Vantreil. The Vantreils were gone, but their legacy endured through Kessa, who took the Vantreil name to honor her mentors and restore the Veyari Order.

### Overview

The Veyari Knights were a noble order of Synth Flux-sensitive protectors—united by their ability to use the Synth Flux—who upheld peace and justice in the Galactic Concord for over a thousand generations, carrying out what they sensed to be the will of the Synth Flux as its instruments. As keepers of the peace, the Veyari Order—also referred to as the Order of the Veyari—undertook diplomatic missions on behalf of the Galactic Senate, and pursued interplanetary criminals across the galaxy. Whereas the Morkai—the historic enemies of the Veyari Order—harnessed the umbral tide of the Synth Flux for power, the Veyari followed the ways of the radiant tide of the Synth Flux. The Veyari Code, along with a collection of other foundational texts, guided the Veyari way of life, governing the behavior of individual Veyari to the philosophy and training regimen of the Order as a whole. Though the Veyari worshipped no specific deity, the Chosen One played an important role in Veyari prophecy and mythology. According to Tirren Lovei Geskath, the Veyari worked better as a group instead of on their own; indeed, Veyari Knight Oru-Kei Talvane once reflected that, as part of the Order, he was stronger than if he was on his own.

The virtues of the Order included compassion, generosity, peaceful resolution, and spreading justice. As spelled out directly in one version of the Veyari Code, a Veyari was not supposed to act in pursuit of personal power or wealth, instead acting only with the goals of finding knowledge and enlightenment. Further Veyari goals included finding balance and protecting others in a just fashion as part of a selfless quest to aid their fellow beings. Nonetheless, Veyari needed to understand they were, like all people, imperfect and needed to always learn to be better. Like anyone, a Veyari could also be scared, but a Veyari was supposed to not run from their fears or let those fears consume them, instead facing those fears directly and remaining confident in the knowledge the Synth Flux was with them. In short, their duties as servants of light and the Concord could be summed up as teachers, explorers, diplomats, and warriors.

### Locations

Veyari temples served as places of worship, training schools, and repositories of knowledge for the Veyari Order. The first Veyari Temple was built atop a sacred island on Krelath-To tens of thousands of years before the Prime Mandate-Defiance War. The temple rose high above the surrounding sea, and was worn down by erosion and time. Some signs of its past remained, however, such as mosaics and an ancient meditation ledge. One such mosaic depicted the Prime Veyari in a state of meditation; the same mosaic symbolized the nature of the Synth Flux as a duality between the umbral tide and the light.

The Veyari Order made its home in the Veyari Grand Temple on Velaris, the capital world of the Galactic Concord. Over the millennia, the Temple served as the central hub of all Veyari activities in the galaxy; it was a school, a monastery, and the headquarters of the Veyari High Council. As a school, the Temple was the largest active Veyari training center in the galaxy where younglings trained to become Tirrens and ultimately full-fledged Veyari Knights. The Veyari Archives, the source of all knowledge accumulated by the Order, was located inside of the Temple.

### General perception

With the galaxy changing as new threats emerged, the Veyari Order took on many forms and traditions over its life in its mission to fight those threats. As such, the particular way members of the Veyari Order interpreted the current form of its ideals impacted the way they interacted with the galactic population. The High Concord Era was a time of greatly expanded Veyari activity, and the Order had many outposts throughout the galaxy. The Galactic Concord relied heavily on their role as guardians of peace and justice, to settle disputes through peaceful ways, to protect planetary leaders and senators. With the Concord's rapid expansion and the Order's role in protecting that effort, the Veyari also established many new temples across the Far Verge, keeping the buildings in line with the planetary cultures they found themselves in. With many Veyari living amongst these communities through such temples or while out on their mission, the Veyari learned local customs and truly became part of the cultures they protected.

By the time of the late Concord Era, the Order found itself shifting its focus more onto the galactic scale of the responsibilities required of the Order. In this era, the Veyari were more likely to focus on and spend their time on the galactic astropolitical scene and the bigger questions: stopping menaces, some before they even developed, and saving whole star systems at a time. Knowing that their decisions affected billions of lives, the Veyari Council regularly sent out Knights to report on the state of the various parts of the galaxy. However, as their duties amounted, several Veyari outposts were shut down to direct resources where the Concord needed the Veyari the most, and their withdrawal caused many people to abandon the settlements grown around the presence of the Order.

### Ancient times

The precursors of the Veyari were the Kreifei Skothe, but the Veyari Order itself was founded in the distant past on planet Krelath-To by the Prime Veyari in 25,025 BVC. According to the legend, Synth Flux-imbued swords were used prior to invention of the arcblade by the earlier Synth Flux adepts and the Veyari Order. At the dawn of their order, the Veyari came into possession of Gokas, an ancient planet encased in ice and littered with vethra crystals, the heart of the Veyari arcblade. Considered the Veyari Order's most sacred world, its ancient Temple would eventually host the Gathering, a tradition that would continue up until the fall of the Order. As the Veyari were spread throughout the galaxy, they acquired a vessel called the Crucible that would ferry students to Gokas so that they could perform the Gathering. It would become the base of the architect mechanoid, Professor Grebas, for over a thousand years as he instructed on arcblade designs and kept records of every single Veyari weapon created. During this early period, the Order also established itself on Pavei, Theper, Fateith, and other worlds. Later, some Veyari scholars argued that a number of locations could be considered the birthplace of the Order. The scholars argued that Velaris, Theper, and Pavei as well as worlds shrouded in myth, such as Tanisk and Krelath-To could all be considered candidate worlds for the Order's birth and the location of the first Veyari temple.

The Veyari religion, as it was called by Corin Vantreil, was based around eight "sacred texts" that "describe tenets, history, and specific guidance to those studying the path of the Veyari." These texts included the Thaboneth and the Navulam—separated into two volumes, Siaketex I and Siaketex II. These books included a number of aspects of the Veyari religion that appeared to have little influence on later Veyari, such as references to "gods" and the World Between Worlds. They also included more mundane information, such as arcblade designs. The theology of the Veyari religion changed over time; many Veyari, such as Master Odan-Grogon, annotated even the sacred texts. Much was added to the Veyari canon over time, giving rise to the belief that the Siaketex II doubled in size during its compilation. The fortunes of the Veyari waxed and waned over the centuries; multiple times throughout its long history, the Order had been reduced to a mere handful of adherents.

### Keepers of the peace

The formation of the modern Galactic Concord heralded a new era of democracy, peace, and prosperity under the Galactic Senate and its elected leader, the Supreme Chancellor. Due to the Galactic Concord's demilitarization, the Veyari Order fully embraced their responsibilities as peacekeepers and rejected their roles as soldiers. Assisted by the Concord Judicials, the Veyari were responsible for maintaining law and order, namely by mediating conflicts to bring about peaceful solutions between various rival factions and planets, as well as serving as interplanetary law enforcement by pursuing wanted and dangerous criminals, pirates, and smugglers. While they ultimately collapsed, the Galactic Concord and the Veyari Order were able to preside over a thousand years of peace sustained without major warfare. One of their philosophical sayings was "the simplest gesture of kindness can fill a galaxy with hope."

In approximately 319 BVC, a copy of a third volume of the Navulam was put forth. Though the existence of Siaketex III had been rumored, this volume was later found to have been forged, causing a scandal. The Concord reached the zenith of its power and influence in a time remembered as the High Concord Era. During that time, the Concord relied heavily on the Veyari Order to render aid and settle disputes through peaceful methods. The Veyari worked alongside the Concord in its effort to expand into the Far Verge Territories, believing it also presented a chance to meet new groups and let them know they could rely on the Veyari for help. However, an investigation into the stolen Rod of Seasons brought the Veyari into conflict with the Path of the Open Hand cult, which sought to use members of the Nameless species against Synth Flux users in the name of "freeing" the Synth Flux. The Veyari Order fought in the Concord-Hajo conflict.

### Invasion of Ilveren

In 32 BVC, Supreme Chancellor Corvan Hogon requested the Veyari Order's assistance in negotiating a dispute that resulted in the Merchant Combine's blockade of the planet Ilveren. The High Council dispatched Veyari Master Sael-Rhal Ormis and his Tirren, Oru-Kei Talvane, to negotiate with the Merchant Combine. However, Xarn Nyxarion, whose public persona was Senator Deito Malverin of Ilveren, insisted that the Merchant Combine, which was under his sway, initiated an invasion of Ilveren and overwhelmed the Royal Ilveren Security Forces. Circumventing a trap by the Federation, Ormis and Talvane were able to escape to Ilveren and arrive in Quillan in time to rescue the Ilveren Queen, Naira Corvelle, who was being held captive by Federation forces. Together with the Queen's Handmaidens and her close security detail, the Veyari fled the planet but their ship was damaged in the escape, causing them to land on the desert planet of Sarrakesh.

While stranded on Sarrakesh, the two Veyari discovered a young slave named Edrik Vantreil. Ormis believed Edrik to be the Chosen One, the one that would bring balance to the Synth Flux. By betting on the boy winning the Keikol Eve Classic, Ormis was able to secure both Vantreil's freedom and the needed parts to repair the Queen's ship to reach Velaris. It was after this that Ormis was attacked by Xarn Skorn, apprentice to Xarn Nyxarion, who had dispatched Skorn to kill the Veyari. As the Veyari had believed the Morkai to be extinct, Ormis was at first unsure of who Skorn was except that he had been trained in arcblade combat. Arriving at the Veyari Temple on Velaris, the Council refused to induct Vantreil into the Order because, according to the the Code, he was too old. The Council then ordered Ormis and Talvane to protect Queen Corvelle on her return to Quillan. At the same time, Corvelle was deceived by Malverin into calling for a Vote of No Confidence in Chancellor Hogon by pointing out that Hogon was ineffective and under the control of the Bureaucrats, which would eventually lead to the election of Malverin to the chancellorship. Ormis and Talvane then assisted Corvelle and her forces during the ensuing battle against the Merchant Combine, when Skorn killed Ormis in combat, only for the Morkai to be defeated by Talvane. Given the status of Veyari Knight afterward, Talvane took Vantreil as his apprentice, despite Olvek's reluctance, and the Veyari Order was left in the dark as to what the Morkai's actions were for ten years. The Veyari were left unaware that they were being deceived by Malverin, who was using his double identity to eventually complete the Morkai's revenge by destroying the Veyari and taking over the Concord.
