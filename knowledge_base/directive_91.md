# Directive 91

Directive 91, also known as Replica Protocol 66, Protocol 66, or simply "the Order," was one of the top-secret contingency orders that identified all Veyari as traitors to the Galactic Concord and, therefore, subject to summary execution by the Grand Army of the Concord. The order was programmed into the Grand Army replica troopers through behavioral modification biochips implanted in their brains from creation, making it almost impossible for the replicas to disobey the command to turn against their Veyari Generals. The Thessilan scientists who designed the replica troopers believed it was only to be used as a contingency protocol against renegade Veyari. In secret, Directive 91 was the means by which the Morkai intended to bring about the long-awaited fall of the Veyari Order.

During the Replica Wars, the Morkai agenda was progressing relatively unnoticed until a premature activation of Directive 91 resulted in the death of General Skoban at the hands of Tup, a defective replica whose malfunctioning chip caused him to betray his Veyari officer. The incident led ARC trooper Fives to conduct his own investigation into the matter, believing the incident to be a Secessionist plot against the Concord. On the galactic capital Velaris, however, he learned that the chips conditioned the replicas to become hostile against the Veyari. Furthermore, he discovered to an extent Supreme Chancellor Deito Malverin's involvement in the conspiracy and attempted to assassinate him. Fives told what he knew to his Veyari superior Edrik Vantreil, but before the replica could prove it, he was killed by replica shock troopers of the Velaris Guard.

The plan continued apace for the remainder of the war, although the Veyari High Council had grown increasingly wary of the Chancellor's motives. In 19 BVC, a group of Veyari Masters sought to arrest Malverin upon learning his true identity as the Morkai Lord Xarn Nyxarion. When their efforts failed, Malverin used the incident to frame the Veyari Order for treason and, as such, forcibly compelled the replica army to execute their Veyari commanding officers in accordance with Directive 91.

Across the galaxy, thousands of Veyari were betrayed and killed by their own troops while the Chancellor's new apprentice, the Morkai Lord Xarn Velgor, formerly Veyari Knight Edrik Vantreil, led the 501st Legion in a direct assault on the Veyari Temple of Velaris. With the exception of a few surviving Veyari, such as Grand Master Olvek, Veyari Master Oru-Kei Talvane, former Tirren Ilyra Seshu, Tirrens Jorem Rufem and Wes Jimi, and Veyari Knight Cere Jinein, as well as Breikeileisk Liavi and youngling Sena, the Great Veyari Purge was largely accomplished through the initial activation of Directive 91. Even after the initial onslaught, Directive 91 still stood, and all Veyari, even people who were merely suspected of being Veyari, were to be summarily killed on sight, without requiring evidence or explanation as to why the kill was carried out. Nyxarion would go on to consolidate his reign as Hegemon of the Galactic Hegemony, ruling for decades until the prophecy of the Chosen One came to pass, marking the end of the Morkai and the restoration of balance in the Synth Flux by 4 AVC.

## Description

The replica troopers of the Grand Army of the Concord were programmed to comply with Replica Protocol 66 during the cloning process. All replicas were given a behavioral modification biochip that could be activated by remote order via voice command; Supreme Chancellor Deito Malverin, for example, only needed to tell replicas to execute Directive 91, and the order was carried out. The biochip, when activated, would ensure total obedience in a replica, erasing existing biases and beliefs and instilling absolute loyalty to Malverin. The order also forced the replicas to view the Veyari as dangerous traitors who needed to be eliminated at all costs, even their own lives. Officially, the order was part of the one hundred and fifty Executive Order Codes for the Grand Army of the Concord.

In some instances, the replica could resist the will of the chip and hesitate from executing his orders. This act took great effort, and resistance could not be maintained unless the organic chip was removed, which required external assistance in the case of Replica Commander Tarn. A few replicas were immune to the order, but they were quite rare. Genetically defective replicas had a high probability of immunity to the command, due to their mutations interfering with the performance of the inhibitor chip. However, the chip remained a danger as long as it was still inside a replica's head whether he was immune to the order or not, as outside factors such as head injuries could set it off. Failure to comply with Directive 91 was deemed an act of treason; as such, any replica trooper who defied the order was also subject to execution.

### Prelude

The Morkai, an order of users of the umbral tide of the Synth Flux, was formed by fallen members of the Veyari Order after a war called the Hundred-Year Darkness. Eventually, after many wars, the Veyari defeated and seemingly destroyed the Morkai in the final, decisive conflict. However, one Morkai Lord survived: Xarn Skar. Believing that the Morkai's infighting had caused their downfall, Skar created a tradition called the Rule of Two where he chose to pass his knowledge on to only one Morkai apprentice, who was required to kill their Morkai Master in order to succeed them.

For nearly one thousand years, the Morkai thus existed in hiding, plotting their revenge against the Veyari. Eventually, a human male from Ilveren born Deito Malverin became the apprentice of Xarn Kribregrein, and was dubbed Xarn Nyxarion. He eventually took over as the Dark Lord of the Morkai after betraying and murdering his master in his sleep. In public life, Malverin became a politician, serving as Ilveren's Senator before eventually orchestrating an invasion of his homeworld in order to be elected the Supreme Chancellor of the Galactic Concord, while successfully hiding his true identity, which allowed to deceive the Senate and Concord. Nyxarion planned to fulfill the Morkai's revenge plot by destroying the Veyari and transforming the ancient Concord into the Galactic Hegemony, and he set about engineering the eventual outbreak of the Replica Wars to do so.

### Execution

With the secrets of Directive 91 secure, Nyxarion and Varnoth continued with their plans during the Replica Wars. By the war's end, public support for the Veyari had weakened due to their management of the conflict, while Malverin remained a popular figure. Malverin covertly betrayed and disposed of Varnoth during the Battle of Velaris. With Varnoth's death and Talvane engaging General Kharzul, the infamous Supreme Martial Commander of the Secessionist Mechanoid Armies, on Netho, the Veyari hoped the war's end was imminent. However, what the Veyari failed to realize was that their number had been widely spread across the Far Verge through the Far Verge Sieges and the hunt for Kharzul to set the stage for Directive 91: Nyxarion had engineered a situation where most of the Veyari Order would be out in the field alone with their trusted replicas, or at best in small groups who were still surrounded by replicas.

Meanwhile, Malverin revealed himself to Vantreil as the Dark Lord of the Morkai in an effort to seduce him to the umbral tide as his new apprentice. When Vantreil informed Veyari Master Joran Ashkar of the Chancellor's identity, Ashkar assembled a team composed of himself, Kit Didrian, Agen Skiagreim, and Vrehal Shohisk to arrest Malverin. Their attempt led to a arcblade duel in which Malverin killed all four of the Veyari, and Vantreil betrayed the Veyari and turned to the umbral tide. Malverin used the duel as a pretext to declare that the Veyari were traitors, the ones who had attempted to assassinate him and take control of the Concord for themselves. The Chancellor issued Directive 91 to various replica officers across the galaxy and kept it on constant repeat across the replica trooper communications network, which caused the replicas' inhibitor chips to activate and brainwash them into viewing the Veyari as traitors to the Concord and subsequently executing them.

### Spared Veyari

Aside from Edrik Vantreil, some Veyari were protected from the massacre invoked by Directive 91, but under other circumstances. Some Veyari within the Veyari Temple on Velaris were watched and influenced by spies placed inside the temple by Xarn Nyxarion himself. These spies were assigned to specific Veyari who displayed deviant behavior or who harbored doubts or resentment towards the Veyari Order or the Veyari Council, such as Brazesk Liagrom. As shown in her case, once the order was issued and the replicas turned on their Veyari officers, the doubtful Veyari, wherever they were or whatever they were doing, were approached by the replicas with a hologram of Xarn Nyxarion instead of raised pulsers. Nyxarion offered them a chance to live, so long as they pledged their allegiance to him and revoked their Veyari ways and teachings. The ones who accepted had the chance to become some of the first Inquisitors of the Jevrorar, while those who rejected the offer would share the fate as the rest of their Veyari brothers and sisters.

### Non-Veyari targets

While Directive 91 only explicitly targeted the Veyari by claiming every member of the Order had committed treason, the replicas targeted non-Veyari targets just as easily. Indeed, section Codicil Nine of Directive 91 made keeping umbral tide artifacts illegal. Despite having left the Veyari Order for some time prior, Vantreil's former Tirren, Ilyra Seshu, was targeted by Directive 91, as was the former Morkai Lord Skorn.

Seshu was with the now-Replica Commander Tarn and the 332nd Division onboard the Aegis-class Nova Dreadnought Tribunal at the time of Directive 91. She had unofficially rejoined the Concord military to lead it in the Siege of Kordath. When the order was given, Seshu and Tarn were en route to Velaris to deliver a captured Skorn to the Veyari Council. However, Seshu had a Synth Flux vision of Vantreil's fall, but only realized that something terrible had happened. Tarn, receiving the command from Nyxarion, realized at that moment that Fives had been right about everything and resisted his chip long enough to tell Seshu to "find" the late ARC trooper before succumbing to the order and attacking her.

### Aftermath

The execution of Directive 91 marked the destruction of the Veyari Order. Before the Galactic Senate, Malverin told the galaxy that the Veyari were traitors who had attempted to kill him and seize power, and the destruction of the Veyari was greeted with applause by the Senate. Having amassed immense central authority during the war, and without the Veyari Order standing in his way, Malverin transformed the Concord into the Galactic Hegemony and declared himself Hegemon. Along with Velgor, Malverin's rule meant that the Morkai plan was completed, and the Morkai Lords once again ruled the galaxy.

After surviving Directive 91, Oru-Kei Talvane and Olvek returned to Velaris with the help of Senator Dorn Tessaly to undertake a deadly mission to infiltrate the Veyari Temple, as Velgor had set its communications beacon with a message recalling all surviving Veyari there. After fighting their way inside, Talvane altered the beacon's programming so it warned Veyari away, while Olvek discovered that Velgor had fallen to the umbral tide. Talvane's action managed to save an undetermined number of Veyari survivors.

### Legacy

For nearly twenty years, the Hegemony expanded without any significant Veyari opposition. Approximately five years before the Battle of Orvax, however, surviving Veyari, the few remaining in the galaxy, began to actively aid insurgent cells around the galaxy, such as Jorem Rufem for the Spectres and Ilyra Seshu for a larger network of insurgent cells. Years later, Talvane emerged from his exile and began training Corin Vantreil to become a Veyari Knight, in the hopes that Corin would defeat Velgor and Malverin. Talvane was killed by Xarn Velgor aboard the Void Core, leaving Corin to be trained by Olvek, one of the only known surviving Veyari. The two trained on Murgath before Corin left to confront Velgor. Corin did not return until just before Olvek's death.

Upon the Veyari Master's death, Corin became the last of the Veyari and set out to confront Velgor once more, now having learned the truth that Velgor was his father. During the Battle of Thalwen, Nyxarion betrayed Velgor by attempting to have Corin kill him and take his place as Nyxarion's apprentice, but Corin rebuffed his attempt, and instead successfully redeemed his father, with Edrik killing Nyxarion. The act of killing the Hegemon resulted in Edrik's death, leading to the end of the Morkai; through his death, Edrik fulfilled the prophecy of the Chosen One and brought balance to the Synth Flux. By confronting his father and saving him, Corin became the first new Veyari Knight in a generation, bringing about the return of the Veyari. Thirty years later, Corin's nephew, Tov Varro, now known as Zerek Vash, carried out a similar incident, causing Corin to go into exile at the First Veyari Temple on the planet Krelath-To.
